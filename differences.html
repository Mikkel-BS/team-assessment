Methodology & limitations

Two different levels of evidence

The personality section uses a validated short measure. The moral-values section deliberately uses adapted construct-level wording to avoid presenting this site as an authorized reproduction of MFQ-2.

Mini-IPIP personality

Four public-domain IPIP items per Big Five factor. The original Mini-IPIP validation found acceptable reliability and validity for a 20-item short form.

Donnellan et al. (2006) · IPIP public domain

Moral Foundations framework

Care, Equality, Proportionality, Loyalty, Authority, and Purity/Sanctity. MFQ-2 research supports this six-foundation structure; the 18 items here are original adaptations and are not a validated MFQ-2 short form.

Atari et al. / Zakharin & Bates · MFQ-2 framework

Interpret carefully

Personality traits are continuous tendencies, not types. This app reports raw 1–5 scale means and does not use population norms. Neuroticism is displayed as Emotional stability by reversing the Mini-IPIP Neuroticism scale so the label is less stigmatizing.

Moral-priority scores are construct indicators for team discussion only. Because the wording here is adapted and not separately validated, do not compare these scores to published MFQ-2 norms or research samples.

Team diversity is represented with respondent standard deviation. The app does not produce compatibility scores, identify “outliers,” infer subgroups, or show individual members' values.

Stricter privacy rules

Measure individuals privately; interpret differences collectively

  • Raw item answers exist only in page memory and disappear on refresh/close.
  • Exported files contain only 11 scale scores, a schema name, and a version number.
  • No names, IDs, timestamps, demographics, free text, or raw answers are exported.
  • Team results remain hidden until at least five valid profiles are loaded.
  • The LLM prompt contains only team means, standard deviations, and response count.
  • The prompt explicitly forbids political, religious, diagnostic, or individual-level inference.

The person collecting files can still inspect an individual JSON file. For stronger practical anonymity, collect files through a process that does not reveal who submitted which file, and avoid retaining the source files after aggregation.
